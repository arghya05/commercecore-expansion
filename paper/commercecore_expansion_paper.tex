\begin{abstract}
Small language models could support several product-understanding operations within a retailer's infrastructure, but evidence of task accuracy is easily confounded with evidence of generalization or serving efficiency. We study QLoRA adaptation of Qwen3-1.7B for query--product relevance, listing identity, functional relation, technical compatibility, and brand/color extraction. We reconstruct the experiments from committed data, checkpoint histories, training logs, prediction records, and serving traces, and release an offline audit that regenerates the quantitative analysis. On a common 200-listing extraction sample, the dedicated adapter attains brand F1 of \ExtractionBrand{} and color F1 of \ExtractionColor{} under the recorded permissive matching rule; the latter exceeds the strongest tested API comparator by \ExtractionColorDelta{} percentage points. This is a descriptive result: two evaluation texts also occur in training, and missing paired outputs prevent a significance claim. Matching results expose a different problem. Adapter and API relevance scores use different samples; a single-token relevance adapter reaches \SingleAccuracy{} accuracy but only \SingleMacroF{} macro-F1; and \ScenarioTestSeen{} of 28 functional-relation test rows reuse training scenarios. A checkpoint sweep further exposes that test partition to model selection. We formalize the information required for comparable evaluations, demonstrate scorer counterexamples, and bound the paired correctness uncertainty recoverable from aggregate extraction counts. Historical load tests contain no completed inference requests. A separate RunPod follow-up adds paired development evaluations under two prompt interfaces, a text-extraction comparator and bounded HTTP serving measurements; these do not establish final-test generalization or production cost advantage. The evidence supports promising narrow extraction behavior and a reproducible account of evaluation failure modes, rather than universal superiority or a production-ready horizontal model.
\end{abstract}
